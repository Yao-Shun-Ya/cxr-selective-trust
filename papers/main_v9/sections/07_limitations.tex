\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Synthetic degradations.} The 19-cell grid is parametric, not acquisition-real. It gives dose--response control but does not reproduce scanner-specific physics.
\item \textbf{Label provenance.} NIH labels are NLP-derived from reports; PadChest uses a 120-label vocabulary mapped to eight findings, with pneumonia structurally absent. Label noise bounds every AUROC in this paper. The common-label analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:risk}) recomputes the transfer loss over the seven labels both datasets annotate; excluding pneumonia does not change the conclusion.
\item \textbf{Cross-dataset attribution.} NIH and PadChest differ in hospital source, case mix, label mapping, and input geometry at the same time. The common-label analysis removes the structurally missing pneumonia term, and the responsiveness check removes the constant-output explanation, but no design here isolates hospital shift from case-mix shift. We claim dataset-level discrimination collapse, not a quantified hospital-domain penalty.
\item \textbf{Six models, one GPU, one decoding protocol.} Greedy decoding maximizes reproducibility but may understate the output diversity of sampling-based deployments.
\item \textbf{Greedy decoding is not batch-invariant.} Re-running the same 100 images at batch sizes 1, 4, and 8 with a fixed seed changed the raw reply on 12--13\% of images; parsed statuses agreed on 7.96--7.97 of eight labels on average, and the parse rate stayed 1.000. This is an order of magnitude below the degradation effects we report, but we do not claim bitwise reproducibility.
\item \textbf{Cross-family input geometry differs by architecture} (0.20--15.24\,MP on the same image) and cannot be equalized without changing the models. We report it rather than hide it (Table~\ref{tab:geometry}); a controlled sweep over two models and three client-side edges (Section~\ref{sec:results-geometry}) shows that the discrimination ordering is stable but the responsiveness ordering can reverse, so the limitation is quantified rather than removed: the remaining four models are not swept.
\item \textbf{Scope of the cross-modal panel.} Beyond the three chest X-ray cohorts, our external replication spans three additional clinical imaging modalities (brain MRI, retinal OCT and dermoscopy; Sections~\ref{sec:results-frontier}--\ref{sec:results-crossmodal}), but evaluates four open-weight checkpoints (three local, one relay-served) on a six-cell probe grid rather than the full multi-family shift matrix.
\item \textbf{CheXagent's contract is not the same task.} Its 79\% \texttt{not\_mentioned} rate makes its selective metrics non-comparable; we exclude it from comparative selective claims.
\item \textbf{Open-i scoring uses a rule-based labeller, cross-checked against CheXbert.} The two labellers disagree on absolute counts and on the omission ordering (Spearman $-0.49$); the hallucination ordering is preserved in direction ($0.71$). We therefore report only the hallucination ordering as stable and do not claim a universal omission--hallucination trade-off.
\item \textbf{Supervised baseline scope.} The baseline is a single DenseNet-121 trained on the 3,871 in-repository training images, not the full 112k ChestX-ray14 release, so 0.733 is a lower bound on what these labels support. It is enough to separate ``task impossible'' from ``model weak'', which is the claim we make, but it is not a clinical-grade reference.
\item \textbf{Concurrent work and readout scope.} Lotfinia et al.\ evaluate a different and larger system set, including checkpoints larger than the Qwen3-VL-8B studied here, so scale and family are not separated in our panel. Khanmohammadi et al.\ evaluate nine confidence estimators under Learn-then-Test and local-label adaptation; we compare output-derived confidence, entropy and margin without local labels, and do not train a correctness probe. Both are explicit scope limits, not negative results.
\item \textbf{Second reasoning model: coverage and budget.} The Qwen3-VL-Thinking grid yields a usable answer on 9,381 of 10,542 cells (89.0\%) at a 5,120-token budget, or 9,401 (89.2\%) after an 8,192-token sample of the residual; the remaining 1,141 replies never terminate, and the 8,192 sample rescues half of them at roughly twice the cost per call. We treat non-termination as an observed outcome and report paired recall on commonly answered studies, but the unpaired per-cell recall is biased toward studies the model chose to finish. The trace is not human-validated and we make no claim about its faithfulness, only about its length and termination.
\item \textbf{Scope of the replications.} The schema sweep covers a second medical model and a cross-family model (Qwen3-VL), and the paraphrase control covers two models; the two-axis result still rests on two models with different channels, which is stronger than one but still not a cross-family law. The CheXbert cross-check is a labeller replication, not a human-adjudicated gold standard.
\item \textbf{The frontier arm is one model on a probe grid.} The 9,400-call relay-served probe uses one open-weight checkpoint, four to six cells per image rather than the 22-cell grid, and a relay that hides its reasoning channel, so the output-mode comparison cannot be repeated there. It establishes that the mechanism is not a small-model artefact; it does not estimate a family-level effect. Five CXR cells exhausted the 8,192-token budget and are counted as contract failures.
\item \textbf{No clinical outcome.} Nothing here measures diagnostic benefit or harm.
\item \textbf{LLaVA-Med is a measured exclusion.} It was run on PadChest, Open-i, and a 200-study NIH subset and produced two templates with no finding-level content (on NIH, 98.7\% modal and 0\% structured parse); it is excluded from finding metrics, not from the record. Open-i carries no definite labels, so we cannot test whether even its binary unreadable/normal gate tracks the reference report.
\item \textbf{Exploratory versus confirmatory.} The four reversals are descriptive analyses with pre-declared rules, not pre-registered hypothesis tests. All 18 layer-model cells are reported rather than a selected subset; the transfer deltas are additionally reported under a 16-cell Bonferroni widening; no $p$-value family-wise claim is made elsewhere.
\end{enumerate}
