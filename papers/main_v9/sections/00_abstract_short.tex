Medical vision--language models (VLMs) are summarized by three numbers---robustness
to image degradation, reliance on the image, and transfer across datasets---each
reported as a property of the model. We show that each is a property of the
model--measurement system, and that four unstated preconditions decide whether it is
identifiable: the model must produce variable outputs on real images
(\emph{responsiveness}), the counterfactual must be compared with a same-space null
(\emph{null model}), the operating point must accompany recall (\emph{operating
point}), and the answer contract and output mode must be declared (\emph{contract
coverage}). Across six open-weight VLMs, 270,324 calls, and three chest X-ray layers,
omitting each precondition reverses the conclusion: five models appear image-blind on
PadChest because they emit 1--20 distinct answers for 1,500 images; Qwen3-VL's
apparently strong Open-i grounding is a single refusal string; contrast degradation
raises recall only by moving the operating point; and quantization appears to suppress
reliance only because the model changes output regime. Two results survive: degradation
has separable noticeability and damage axes, and the label-level sensitivity ordering
is model-invariant in raw ranks on NIH but partly confounded by prevalence. A
selective-trust policy fails, covering at most 9.3\% of findings at a 5\% risk budget.
A frozen-encoder linear probe reaches 0.651--0.703 while the prompted readouts sit at
0.50--0.63, locating the loss in the prompted readout rather than the image
representation. Four open-weight models, including a frontier checkpoint served through a relay,
reproduce the cross-modal ordering: dermoscopy is the most responsive of brain MRI,
retinal OCT and dermoscopy for every model, and the null separation falls below zero
for MedGemma-1.5 on OCT after a 12-test correction. The study delivers 326,830 unique
cells (351,329 requests). We release the four checks as a CPU-only audit and argue
that reliance, robustness and transfer be reported with their denominators.
