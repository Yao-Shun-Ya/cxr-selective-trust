# 2. Related Work

## 2.1 Medical vision-language models for chest X-ray

Medical VLMs have rapidly expanded from task-specific classifiers to instruction-following multimodal models. CheXagent introduced a CXR-specific foundation model trained on CheXinstruct, a large multi-task CXR corpus, and demonstrated strong performance across perception, reasoning, and report-generation tasks (Chen et al., 2024). MedGemma extends the Gemma 3 family with medical multimodal pretraining and instruction tuning, including chest X-ray capabilities (Sellergren et al., 2025). General-domain models such as Qwen2.5-VL provide strong open-weight visual recognition, document parsing, and structured output capabilities without medical-specific fine-tuning (Bai et al., 2025). At the same time, negative results have shown that vision-language pretraining does not necessarily produce transferable representations for dense multi-label CXR classification (Fisher, 2025). This tension between broad multimodal capability and fine-grained medical reliability motivates our joint evaluation of discrimination, calibration, and image reliance.

## 2.2 Calibration and uncertainty in medical AI

Calibration measures whether predictive confidence matches empirical correctness. In medical imaging, miscalibration can be clinically dangerous because overconfident errors may escape human review. CALM-VLM integrated temperature scaling and selective abstention into a generative 3D VLM for brain MRI classification, improving coverage-adjusted ROC-AUC across multi-site data (Dhinagar et al., 2026). Calibration for medical VQA has also been studied empirically and through verbalized uncertainty training (MICCAI 2025; Just how sure are you, 2026). However, these works primarily target single-modality or internal settings and do not test whether calibration transfer survives image-quality shift. Our work evaluates calibration after source-domain fitting and applies it to shifted and external CXR data, while explicitly conditioning reliability on visual evidence use.

## 2.3 Selective prediction and abstention

Selective prediction allows a model to abstain on uncertain cases, trading coverage for lower risk. Recent work has formalised fine-grained abstention for medical VLMs, including answer, clarify, and abstain actions under image corruption, text corruption, and cross-modal mismatch (Nguyen et al., 2026). AT-CXR combined uncertainty estimates with an agentic triage policy on NIH ChestX-ray14 and achieved strong risk–coverage performance for pulmonary edema triage (Li et al., 2025). These results establish the promise of selective prediction. Our work differs in two ways. First, we evaluate multi-label CXR findings under a systematic image-quality shift benchmark rather than a single balanced triage task. Second, and more importantly, we show that selective risk–coverage can appear favourable for a model that is image-invariant, motivating image-reliance-aware selective trust.

## 2.4 Distribution shift and robustness

Distribution shift in medical imaging arises from differences in scanners, acquisition protocols, patient populations, and post-processing. Prior work has documented domain shift and dataset bias in public CXR datasets (Rafferty et al., 2026), and robustness studies have shown that VLMs are sensitive to medical image artefacts (Cheng et al., 2025). Stress-testing studies have further revealed that multimodal medical benchmarks can overstate readiness when critical visual inputs are removed (Gu et al., 2025). Our shift benchmark complements these efforts by measuring performance, calibration, and image sensitivity across six image-quality axes and three severity levels. Our results show that shift effects are heterogeneous and non-monotonic, suggesting that robustness cannot be summarised by a single degradation curve.

## 2.5 Image reliance, grounding, and hallucination

A central question for medical VLMs is whether predictions are grounded in image content or driven by language and label priors. Studies using blank-image substitution have found substantial variability in visual dependency across frontier models (Felizzi et al., 2025). Large-scale analyses of multimodal medical models have shown that image predictions can be dominated by text and that misleading clinical vignettes can sharply reduce accuracy (Buckley et al., 2026). Counterfactual evaluations with real, blank, and shuffled images have introduced metrics such as visual reliance score, image sensitivity, and hallucinated visual reasoning rate (Zafar et al., 2026). For radiology report generation, hallucination benchmarks and mitigation methods have begun to characterise factual inconsistencies (Heiman et al., 2025; MedVH, 2025). Our work connects this literature to selective trust: we treat image reliance as a first-class reliability signal and show that a model can be image-invariant while producing valid structured outputs and apparently acceptable selective metrics.

## 2.6 Positioning

Existing work has studied medical VLM calibration, selective prediction, robustness, and grounding largely in isolation. Calibration and selective prediction have been advanced on MRI and CXR triage (Dhinagar et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2026). Robustness and grounding studies have shown that image quality and visual dependency matter (Cheng et al., 2025; Buckley et al., 2026; Zafar et al., 2026). Our work is the first, to our knowledge, to jointly evaluate calibration, uncertainty, selective prediction, shift response, and image reliance for open-source CXR VLMs, and to demonstrate that image-invariant models can be selectively trusted by conventional metrics. This motivates the shift-aware selective trust framework developed in this paper.
