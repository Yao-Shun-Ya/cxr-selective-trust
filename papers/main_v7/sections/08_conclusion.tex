\section*{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The field has learned to report robustness, reliance, and transfer as model properties. Our data say they are properties of the model--measurement system. Four cheap checks---responsiveness, a same-space null, the operating point, and contract coverage---reverse the headline conclusion in each of the three layers we studied. Two findings survive the checks: degradation has two separable descriptive axes, and the label-level hierarchy of sensitivity is model-invariant on NIH in raw ranks, though partly confounded by prevalence and still in need of external replication. Four confirmatory experiments sharpen both findings rather than merely repeating them. A supervised baseline at macro AUROC 0.733 shows that chance-level VLM discrimination is a model property, and the same CNN reproduces the contrast-induced operating-point movement under a fixed rule, so that reversal is not specific to vision-language models; the schema sweep and paraphrase control show that the contract and the wording jointly decide what a model says about an image with no evidence in it; and a second reasoning model reproduces the operating-point mechanism while relocating the silent quadrant: noise is invisible to MedGemma-1.5's marker channel and plainly visible to a channel that watches whether reasoning terminates. Silent failure therefore belongs to the monitor, not to the corruption---which is the practical form of the paper's thesis. The selective-trust framework we set out to validate does not survive: its components barely change error ranking and yield no usable coverage at a 5\% risk budget (at most 9.3\% of findings).

The constructive claim is modest and, we think, more useful than a leaderboard: report the denominator before the number. Table~\ref{tab:checklist} turns the four preconditions into a checklist that any benchmark can apply to an existing prediction file.

\section*{Reproducibility}

All artifacts are released with the manuscript. The responsiveness audit and the selective-input adapter are CPU-only scripts; every figure is generated from the frozen prediction files rather than from transcribed numbers. The primary run state (38 stage entries: 30 completed, of which 8 are flagged contract-exempt for structured-contract metrics---six Open-i stages and two LLaVA stages---while Open-i prose metrics are still scored) is recorded in a resumable state file, and the preprocessing contract---including the per-model effective input geometry---is documented separately. The all-inclusive call count is 275,618: 270,324 for the six-model matrix and 5,294 for LLaVA-Med (3,000 PadChest, 1,894 Open-i, 400 NIH subset). Three model-specific environments were used: \texttt{.venv-vl} (Python 3.10, torch 2.5.1+cu121, transformers 4.51.3) for MedGemma, Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL; \texttt{.venv-chexagent} (torch 2.1.0) for CheXagent; and \texttt{.venv-modern} (torch 2.14.1+cu126, transformers 5.18.0) for Qwen3-VL. Frozen dependency locks are in \texttt{program/env/lock-*.txt}. Scripts 75--101 produce the CSV artifacts; figures are generated by script 80; main-text tables are checked against those CSVs by script 104. The 41-stage coverage census reports 284,695 delivered calls: the 270,324-call six-model matrix, 9,477 calls for the second reasoning model (9,401 usable cells plus 76 smoke calls), and 4,894 LLaVA-Med calls on PadChest and Open-i; the 400-study LLaVA-Med NIH subset is outside the 41 delivered stages, so the difference from the all-inclusive total is exactly 9,077 calls. A split-pure 1,506-row evaluation manifest (with SHA-256 in a manifest lock file) is included so the scored set and its denominator are auditable. A CPU-only crash/resume test (40 checks, stubbed model) and the \texttt{cxr\_trust} unit tests run without a GPU. Dataset licenses remain those of the original releases (NIH ChestX-ray14, PadChest, Open-i), and model checkpoints are used under their upstream licenses.
