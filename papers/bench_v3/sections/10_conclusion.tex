\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A benchmark number is only as trustworthy as the harness that produced it --- and, more specifically, as reconstructible as its denominator. We presented a harness for a three-layer medical VLM study (38 stages, 275,218 calls, 39 frozen prediction files) and, unlike the usual self-report, measured it against four alternatives on the same workload.

Two numbers summarise the result. The durability guarantee costs 1.80\,ms per cell, about eight minutes across the whole study, and it buys zero lost rows after a crash where naive append loses 1.4\%, and loud refusal where two unsupervised writers would otherwise produce 500 to 699 duplicate keys. Neither number is dramatic on its own; together they turn ``durable'' from an adjective into a trade with a price and a payoff.

We also replaced the unfalsifiable claim of zero error rows with five invariants that a hostile tester can break, and released a census that recomputes a file's denominator from the file itself. Applied to 259 prediction files it returns a non-trivial answer: four non-uniform denominators and one file with duplicate keys. An audit that cannot fail is not an audit. We hope the checklist in Table~\ref{tab:checklist} travels: silent collapse, duplicate rows after resume, schema compliance mistaken for coverage, and unmeasured batch collapse are generic to large medical VLM evaluations, not specific to chest X-rays.
